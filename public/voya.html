UX/UI Design · Onboarding Redesign

VOYA

A lifestyle app that organizes your saved reels from social media into real travel plans.

Role
UX Designer
Type
Onboarding Redesign
Status
In Development

Overview

I joined VOYA's team to redesign their onboarding, building on a first version that was made with AI.

The Problem

The original onboarding didn't work

The original onboarding was built with AI and shipped fast. It didn't show the lifestyle the app promises, and it asked too much before giving anything back.

Missing the brand vibe
The visual style didn't show the luxury travel feeling the app is built around.
Questions without purpose
Users answered personal questions with no clear reason and no value in return.
Unclear value, weak paywall
Users reached payment without understanding what they were paying for.

The original flow

The original flow

Three moments in the original flow where users were asked for something before they saw any value.

01

A splash screen that doesn't capture the spirit of the app

02

Several personal questions before showing any value

03

A payment screen before users understood the app

What I found

The questions felt like a survey, not a welcome. By the time users reached the app, the questions had already used up their patience.

The core tension

The founders wanted to ask questions to build investment early. I believed the user needed to feel the app's value first. That tension shaped every screen.

Research

Market research

Before designing, I looked at competitor apps, studied the visual style I wanted to create, and looked at what users need in the first 60 seconds of using an app.

Competitor Analysis

Similar apps + the best in the world

I looked at travel apps and also top apps like Airbnb and Spotify to see what makes a first impression actually work.

Vibe Research

The aesthetic of wanderlust

I studied the visual style of luxury travel - the colors, photos, and fonts that say "this is the life you want."

User Needs Mapping

What users need in 60 seconds

In the first minute, users need to know: what is this, what do I get, and why now. The redesign answers all three.

Design Decisions

Three changes to the flow

Decision 01

Reduced the questions to one

The founders wanted to keep several questions to build investment. I believed most of them weren't worth asking. We agreed on one question, and replaced the rest with short videos showing the real app in use.

Decision 02

Showed value with videos before asking anything

Before asking users to pay, I added a few screens showing the app's real features - the map, the AI trip planner, and what they'd actually get.

Decision 03

Removed the 12-month plan and simplified payment

I wanted the vacation feeling to carry through to checkout, so I redesigned the payment screen to feel less like a form. The founder and I also removed the 12-month plan - it felt like a long commitment for a user who hadn't even taken a trip with the app yet.

Reflection

What I learned

The biggest thing I learned: onboarding should prove value before asking for anything.

The redesign hasn't launched yet, so I don't have data on how it performs - but I can't wait to see the numbers once it's live.
