VOYA

Overview

VOYA is a lifestyle app that collects your saved reels from social media, organizes them on a map, and helps you turn inspiration into real travel plans - with an AI trip planner and personalized recommendations. I joined the startup to redesign the onboarding experience from the ground up.

The Problem

Strong product, weak first impression.

The original onboarding was built with AI and shipped fast - but it wasn't doing its job. It didn't reflect the aspirational lifestyle the app promises, and it was losing users before they even got started.

Missing the brand vibe
The visual language felt generic - it didn't communicate the luxury lifestyle and wanderlust the app was built around.
Questions without purpose
Users were asked multiple personal questions with no clear reason - creating friction without delivering any visible value in return.
Unclear value, weak paywall
Users reached the payment screen without fully understanding what they were paying for - and a 12-month plan made the price feel overwhelming.

How Might We

How might we make the onboarding feel as aspirational and effortless as the lifestyle VOYA promises?

The original flow

Lots of questions. No explanation. No feeling.

Three moments where the original onboarding lost users - before they ever saw what the app could do.

01

No hook, no feeling

A splash screen that didn't communicate what the app is about

02

Questions with no payoff

Multiple personal questions before users understood what they were signing up for

03

A paywall that felt like a wall

Users reached the payment screen without understanding what they were paying for

What I found

Users were dropping off mid-flow. The questions felt like a survey, not a welcome - and by the time they reached the app, some had already lost interest in what it could offer.

The core tension

The product team's need to know the user clashed with the user's need to feel the app first. That conflict lived in every screen - and asking too much, too early, cost them conversions.

Research

I looked at what the best apps do differently.

Before designing, I mapped the competitive landscape, studied the vibe I wanted to create, and identified what matters most to the user in the first 60 seconds of an app.

Competitor Analysis

Similar apps + the best in the world

I analyzed direct competitors in the travel space, and then looked beyond - at Airbnb, Spotify, and other top-tier apps that nail their first impression. The goal: understand what separates a forgettable onboarding from one that actually converts.

Vibe Research

The aesthetic of wanderlust

I explored the visual language of luxury travel - the colors, photography style, and typography that communicate "this is the life you want." That became the visual north star for the redesign.

User Needs Mapping

What users need in 60 seconds

In the first minute, users need to understand: what is this? what do I get? and why now? The original onboarding answered none of these clearly. The redesign was built around answering all three.

Version 02

Show first,
ask less, convert more

Instead of starting with questions, V2 starts with one lightweight moment of connection - then earns commitment by showing users what they'll get. Every screen has one job, and nothing comes before value.

Design Decisions

Three things that changed everything

Decision 01

A welcoming first screen - with far fewer questions

The original flow opened cold and asked too much, too fast. I redesigned the splash screen to immediately communicate the app's vibe and aspirational feel - and cut the questions down to only what's essential, so users move forward without friction.

Decision 02

Show the value before asking for anything

Before asking users to pay or commit, the redesign takes them through the key features - what the map looks like, how the AI planner works, and what they'll actually get. Users arrive at the paywall already convinced.

Decision 03

Sell the lifestyle, not the subscription

The payment screen was redesigned to feel like the destination, not a form. We also removed the 12-month plan - it made the price feel heavy before users were ready. The focus shifted to the experience they're unlocking, not the cost.

Reflection

From AI output to human feeling

The biggest lesson from this project: onboarding isn't about collecting information - it's about making a promise. Every screen should earn the next tap. The moment we stopped asking users for data and started showing them what they'd gain, everything felt better.

The redesign is currently heading into user testing. I'm looking forward to seeing whether the aspiration actually converts.
