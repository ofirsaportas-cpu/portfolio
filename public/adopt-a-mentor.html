Adopt A Mentor

Connecting new adopters with experienced pet parents - so no one has to figure it out alone.

Role
UX / UI Designer
Type
Web → Mobile
Deliverable
Feature Design

Overview

I volunteered as a designer for AdoptMe, a nonprofit community for pet adopters. The brief came from their product manager: design a way to connect new adopters with experienced mentors.

The Problem

Adopting a pet can be overwhelming.

This came up from AdoptMe's own community: adopters have questions that feel too small for a vet, but too important to ignore - and no one to ask.

Information Gap
Questions too minor for a vet but too important to ignore - left completely unanswered.
Emotional Overload
New adopters are overwhelmed and anxious - they need empathy, not just instructions.
No Community
Experienced owners want to help - but there's no platform connecting them to those who need it.

Main Goals

What I wanted the feature to do

#1
Create guidance for adopters
A clear, structured path with human support at every step.
#2
Reduce anxiety in early adoption
Reduce confusion and overwhelm in the critical first weeks.
#3
Build community engagement
Connect experienced owners to those who need them - creating mutual value.

User Personas

Two sides of the same connection

Two users with opposite relationships to the process - one is looking for help, one is offering it. That asymmetry shaped every design decision. I built these personas from real interviews with both kinds of users.

Dana Shimon
The New Adopter

Background

Adopting a dog for the first time. Excited but afraid of making the wrong call - insecure and overwhelmed.

Main Goal

Gain confidence and understand what to expect in the first weeks.

Pain Points

Afraid of making mistakes with her new pet
Emotionally overloaded, hesitant to ask friends
No trusted source for small but important questions
Ofek Levy
The Mentor

Background

Has adopted several dogs over the years. Wants to help new adopters avoid common mistakes.

Main Goal

Make a meaningful impact by sharing his experience in a supportive, structured way.

Pain Points

Limited time - needs a flexible, low-commitment way to help
Difficulty setting clear boundaries with new people
Concern that his help won't be truly valued

V1 - How it worked

I followed the brief and built the first version

Following the product manager's brief, I designed a registration flow that matched adopters to mentors by location only - the mentor could then approve or decline the match.

Landing Page

User selection flow that directs mentors and adopters through the relevant registration process.

New Adopter Registration

Form collects key pet details - type, age, size - to understand the adopter's situation.

Mentor Match Proposal

Mentor receives adopter's profile and can accept the match - then receives their WhatsApp contact.

Design Reflection

What V1 taught me

Key Insight

The first form felt clunky
A usability test showed the form was inefficient for users.
Location wasn't enough
The brief missed mutual choice - adopters needed to approve their mentor too, not just receive one.
Mutual choice
I made registration effortless, and replaced the algorithm with a community pool both sides browse and approve.

Version 2

Mobile-first, visual registration

Decision 01

I replaced open text fields with visual tiles and selectors

I kept typing only where free input was truly necessary. Everything else became tap-to-select tiles, collecting the same information with far less effort.

Decision 02

I turned the form into a guided, tap-through flow

Instead of one static form, users now tap through a sequence of focused screens - each one feels like a short step forward, not a form to fill out.

Decision 03

I split registration into one step at a time

I broke the form into focused steps, each screen asking for one thing at a time, instead of showing the full length upfront.

Key Screens

The full V2 flow

Each step is a separate screen, so users always know what's being asked of them right now.

Reflection

What I learned

User testing showed the real problem wasn't just the form - it was the matching logic itself. Location alone couldn't account for what makes a good mentor relationship, and adopters needed to approve their match, not just receive one.

This was a learning project where I worked through two versions, each one improving on the last. The organization is now testing whether to bring it live - I'm looking forward to seeing how the community responds, and improving it further with that feedback.
